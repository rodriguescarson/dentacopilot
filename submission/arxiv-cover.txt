Abstract (use the one in §0 of paper.tex; ~250 words):

  Commercial dental artificial intelligence in 2026 is overwhelmingly
  diagnostic: caries, calculus, periapical, and bone-level detection on
  radiographs. The clinically harder question — given a patient's
  chart and current procedure context, what should the dentist do next
  — remains unsolved at general-dentistry scale. The closest published
  system, MultiTP (Liu et al., 2024), is a CNN-RNN restricted to
  partial edentulism and provides neither calibrated uncertainty,
  structured rationale, nor an evaluation that treats the model as
  decision support rather than as an autonomous classifier.

  We introduce DentaCoPilot, a recommender that, given a structured
  chart and the most recent procedure, returns a calibrated top-K
  distribution over Current Dental Terminology (CDT) codes for the
  next procedure, a chart-grounded rationale, and an explicit abstain
  signal when context is insufficient. The system is built on a small
  large language model, constrained-decoded to the CDT vocabulary, and
  calibrated via temperature scaling against verbalized confidence.

  Our central contribution is methodological: we evaluate the system
  as a clinician-augmenting consult, not as an autonomous classifier,
  and demonstrate that prompt-conditioning the LLM on a classical
  baseline's top-K candidates closes the LLM-vs-baseline accuracy gap
  on synthetic data. We further report calibration, coverage-risk
  curves, adversarial-input abstention, and a pre-registered
  clinician-in-the-loop evaluation plan for the BigMouth multi-
  institutional repository and the KLE Vishwanath Katti Institute of
  Dental Sciences.

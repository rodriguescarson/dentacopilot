Plain-language summary (for medRxiv) — DentaCoPilot
=====================================================

(Plain-English version of the abstract for non-specialist readers. Aim for
~150 words; medRxiv accepts up to 200.)

----

Most artificial intelligence tools used in dentistry today help dentists
read dental X-rays — they spot cavities, gum-disease bone loss, or
infections in images. None of them help with the harder question that
follows: given everything in a patient's dental record, what should the
dentist do next? Should this tooth get a filling, a root canal, or a
crown? Should this patient come back in three months or six?

We built DentaCoPilot, a software tool that uses a large language model
(an AI like ChatGPT) to suggest the next dental procedure for a patient,
together with a brief explanation grounded in their chart. The tool is
designed to support a dentist's decision, not replace it: it suggests
multiple possibilities ranked by probability, says how confident it is,
and explicitly says "I don't know" when the patient's record is too thin
to be sure.

This paper describes the tool's design, evaluates eight versions of it on
a synthetic dental record dataset, and reports that combining a
traditional statistical model with a language model gives the best
results. Real-patient evaluation at the KLE Vishwanath Katti Institute of
Dental Sciences and the BigMouth multi-school dental record repository is
the next step.
